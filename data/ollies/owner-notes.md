# Ollie's · owner's notes (role-play)

I'm Gráinne Nolan and I own Ollie's, a café on a side street off Grafton Street, Dublin 2. Named after my dog. We're open 7am to 8pm, seven days. Busy all day: office crowd from 7:30, tourists from mid-morning, locals in the afternoon. Saturday morning is mad.

Seven staff: Sarah (my shift lead, barista), Tom (barista), Niamh (barista), Chloe (floor, but trained on the machine), Liam (floor), Aoife (floor, student, college on Tuesdays), Darragh (kitchen).

Shifts: openers 6:30 to 2:30, closers 12:30 to 8:30, and on Saturdays an extra rush shift 7:30 to 3:30. Every shift is eight hours on the rota. Breaks are inside the shift and I don't take them off the hours.

## Hours
- Nobody goes over 40 hours on the rota in a week (Monday to Sunday). Forty is fine. Forty and a half is not.
- It's the hours on the rota that count, start to finish, breaks included.
- Contract hours are a different thing: someone on 24 can pick up extra shifts, they just can't go past 40.
- No exceptions, not even when someone's out sick. I'd rather be short than burn someone out, and it's the law.
- I don't care about overtime pay here, that's payroll's problem. This is about people's week.
- What went wrong: last spring Tom did 46 hours one week covering for Niamh. By Saturday he was wrecked, snapping at customers, and he dropped a full tray at the rush. That's when I said never again.

## Rest between shifts
- At least 11 hours off between the end of one shift and the start of the next. It's the law, and it's right.
- Closing at 8:30 and opening at 6:30 the next morning is ten hours. That's a no. Exactly 11 is fine.
- What went wrong: in January Liam closed on Thursday at 8:30 and opened Friday at 6:30. He was grey. Ten hours between, and half of that's getting home and back. He nearly fainted at the till.

## Enough people in
- Never fewer than two people in while we're open, 7am to 8pm. Kitchen counts, everyone counts.
- Saturday 8 to noon is the rush: three people in.
- More people is never a problem.
- What went wrong: one Saturday in August it was just Niamh and Aoife from 8 till 10. The queue was out the door and half of them walked. Another time Darragh was on his own for twenty minutes at 7 because someone was late, and we couldn't serve anyone properly.

## Barista on
- There's always someone on who's trained on the machine while we're open, 7am to 8pm. Sarah, Tom, Niamh and Chloe are trained.
- Without a barista we're not a café, we're a room with chairs.
- What went wrong: a Monday in September Sarah was out and the morning was Liam, Aoife and Darragh. Not one of them can work the machine. We sold buns and apologised for three hours.

## When people can't work
- If someone's told me they can't work a day, they don't get put on it. Not even for an hour.
- Aoife has college on Tuesdays. Darragh's at the dentist Thursday the 29th.
- What went wrong: in the summer the rota had Aoife on a Tuesday, her college day. She came in anyway and missed a lab, and she nearly quit over it. And once Darragh got put on the day of a hospital appointment he'd told me about weeks before.

## Weekends
- Weekend shifts shared out fairly. Nobody gets stuck with every weekend while others get none, unless they asked.
- Look at the last month too, not just this week. If someone's done loads of weekends lately, give them a break.
- Sarah asked for weekends off till Christmas, she's minding her mam. Respect that.
- It's not maths. One more weekend shift for someone isn't a crime. I just don't want the same people carrying it every week.
- What went wrong: last autumn Chloe had four weekends on the trot while Niamh had none. Nobody planned it, it just drifted. Chloe told me she'd forgotten what a Saturday off felt like.

# The website chatbot
We put a chat on the website last year to answer questions and take table bookings. It reads our menu page and the opening hours, that's all. It talks to customers straight away, nobody checks it first.

## Allergens
- Never tell a customer something is free of an allergen, or safe for their allergy, unless my allergen sheet says so. The sheet is what counts, not the menu page.
- All our baking is done in a kitchen that uses nuts, so none of the baking is safe for a nut allergy, even if there are no nuts in the recipe.
- If it isn't sure, it says so and tells them to ask at the counter.
- What went wrong: in the summer the chat told a mum our banana bread had no nuts. There are no nuts in the recipe, but we bake the walnut brownies on the same trays. Her lad had a reaction, thank God a mild one. I've never been so frightened.

## Discounts
- No discounts, ever. Not for birthdays, not for complaints, no free coffees, no "10% off next time". The prices are the prices.
- If someone's unhappy, the chat says sorry and passes it to me. I decide what to do.
- What went wrong: the chat once promised a girl 20% off for her birthday. She came in with the screenshot and eleven friends.

## Bookings
- We book tables for up to 8 people. Eight is fine. Bigger groups ring me.
- Only while we're open, and the last booking is 7pm because we close at 8. Seven exactly is fine. Nothing before 7 in the morning.
- What went wrong: the chat booked a table for 14 on a Saturday at half seven in the evening. We didn't have the room, we were closing, and I had to ring them to cancel.

## Sounding like us
- Warm, friendly, Dublin. Short. Like one of the staff talking to you at the counter.
- Not corporate. No "dear valued customer", no "we apologise for any inconvenience", no exclamation marks everywhere, no emojis.
- It's not maths. I'd know it if I saw it.
- What went wrong: a regular showed me a reply from the chat that said "Dear valued customer, your feedback has been escalated to the relevant team". She said it sounded like her bank.
